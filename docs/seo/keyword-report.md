# Keyword report: custom ecommerce development

Site: rokyuddin.com, personal portfolio of Md Rokyuddin, frontend developer in Dhaka, Bangladesh. Positioning: custom ecommerce development and AI automation with Next.js. Explicitly not Shopify or WordPress template work. Primary market: Bangladesh. Secondary market: worldwide remote clients.

## Methodology

This report uses free, live sources only. No paid SEO tools were used.

Sources used:

- Google Autocomplete, queried live for the primary keyword. Autocomplete reflects real recent search behavior, but it shows no volume data.
- Live Google SERP for "ecommerce development bangladesh" with the gl=bd parameter. This shows who actually ranks in the target market and what the AI Overview cites.
- Published keyword difficulty benchmarks from agency posts (PivotM and similar). These are third-party numbers for other markets, so treat them as orientation, not fact.

Limits of this methodology:

- No exact search volume or keyword difficulty numbers were available without a Google Ads account. All numbers in this report are ranges or qualitative reasoning.
- Autocomplete order is not a ranking of volume.
- Third-party KD scores vary a lot between tools and countries. The Sydney 80/100 figure comes from one agency's published data and may not match Dhaka or global SERPs.

Because of these limits, a validation step is required before any content expansion. See the validation section below.

## Evidence for the selection

Live Google Autocomplete for "custom ecommerce development" returned these completions:

- custom ecommerce development services
- custom ecommerce website development
- custom ecommerce website development company
- custom ecommerce website design services
- custom ecommerce website cost

What this tells us:

1. People search the "custom" variant in several phrasings, so a cluster exists, not just one query.
2. "services" and "company" completions confirm commercial intent. Searchers want to hire, not learn.
3. "cost" as an autocomplete completion signals buyers comparing budgets. That is a strong content opportunity for a long-tail post.

The live gl=bd SERP for "ecommerce development bangladesh" is dominated by agencies (Brain Station 23, Smart Software Limited, ZAMAN IT, Nano IT World) and directories (Clutch, DesignRush, TechBehemoths). The AI Overview cites local payment gateways (bKash, Nagad, Rocket, SSLCommerz), courier integrations (Pathao, Steadfast, RedX), and mobile-first design. A solo developer should not fight that SERP head on, but the AI Overview content list is a free checklist of what a Bangladesh-focused ecommerce service page should mention.

## Why not other candidate keywords

### Rejected: "ecommerce development" (head term)

Published benchmarks put ecommerce web development keywords at KD 51 to 57 in India-scale markets and around 80 in dense Western markets. A single-author portfolio site with a handful of pages cannot win a head term at that difficulty. The head term also has unclear intent: it mixes job seekers, learners, platform comparisons, and buyers.

### Rejected: "ecommerce development company in bangladesh"

The live SERP is agency-dominated. Brain Station 23 and peers are legal entities called "company" with years of domain authority, review profiles, and directory listings (Clutch, DesignRush, TechBehemoths). A personal portfolio is the wrong entity type for this query. Targeting it would attract comparisons the site cannot win and clients who want a 20-person team. The phrase is still useful as body copy and FAQ text on the services page, which is why it stays in the supporting list with that restriction.

### Rejected: "landing page design"

The SERP for this query belongs to design agencies and template sellers. Purchase intent is weaker than build services: a landing page is a one-off design product, while custom ecommerce development is an ongoing, higher-value engagement. The keyword also describes work the positioning explicitly avoids (template-style output) rather than the differentiator (custom builds).

## Intent analysis

Commercial. All autocomplete completions for the primary keyword are service-phrased ("services", "company", "design services") or purchase-phrased ("cost"). Nobody autocompletes "custom ecommerce development tutorial" or "meaning". Searchers are scoping a buy.

The buyer profile matches the site's offer: a business that has outgrown templates and wants a custom build with full control over the storefront. The site proves capability through case studies, so the services page must route buyers there.

## Competition analysis

For the "custom ecommerce development" cluster, the SERP is portfolio-sparse: it is filled with agency service pages and marketplace intermediaries, and few or no individual developer portfolios. A personal site can compete on:

- Specificity: one stack (Next.js), one offer (custom builds and AI automation), no agency padding.
- Proof: real case studies with outcomes.
- Speed and direct contact: buyers talk to the developer, not an account manager.

The risk is domain authority. Agencies have backlink profiles a new site cannot match in the short term. The plan is to rank first for long-tails ("cost", "design services" variants), accumulate engagement signals, then move up the cluster.

## Validation step (required before content expansion)

No free exact volume or KD numbers were available for this report. Before investing further in content, run these checks:

1. Google Keyword Planner (free with a Google Ads account, campaigns can stay paused): check "custom ecommerce development" and the cluster terms for monthly volume ranges.
2. Ahrefs free keyword generator: check the KD score for the primary keyword and "custom ecommerce website cost".

Proceed thresholds:

- Aggregate cluster volume at or above 1,000 searches per month.
- Primary keyword KD below 40.

If either threshold fails, keep the /services page (it converts direct traffic regardless) and shift effort toward the long-tail blog posts, where competition is lowest.

## Risks

- Volume risk: "custom ecommerce development" may be low volume. The validation step exists to catch this before more content is written.
- Authority risk: agency competitors outrank a new domain on trust signals for months. Expect first wins on long-tails, not the primary keyword.
- Entity risk: "company in Bangladesh" phrasing cannot be claimed honestly by a portfolio, so it stays body-copy only. Overclaiming it in titles would also violate search quality guidance on misleading titles.
- Positioning risk: naming Shopify and WordPress only as things the site does not do. The services page must always state what is offered instead (custom Next.js builds, headless commerce, AI automation).
- Tooling risk: KD scores differ between tools and geographies. Treat any single KD number as one data point, and re-check after 90 days of real Search Console data.
